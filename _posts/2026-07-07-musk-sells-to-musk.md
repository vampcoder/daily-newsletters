The boom is eating labor, sleep, benchmarks, GPUs, and apparently its own expense policy.

## Musk Sells To Musk

Late on Friday, Elon Musk announced that xAI has acquired X (formerly Twitter) in an all-stock transaction. The deal values xAI at $33 billion and X at $33 billion, effectively merging the two companies. Musk said the combined entity's valuation could reach $80 billion.

This is a merger of convenience rather than a strategic union. X brings a built-in user base of hundreds of millions and a real-time data feed; xAI brings cutting-edge AI models, like Grok, and a team of top-tier researchers. The deal could give xAI a distribution channel for its AI products and X an injection of AI-powered features.

But the optics are interesting. Musk is effectively selling X to himself—through a company he controls—and calling it a merger. Questions about corporate governance, debt, and minority shareholders will likely surface. Still, Musk now owns an integrated AI-social powerhouse, the first of its kind.

## Agents Never Clock Out

A quiet revolution is happening in AI: agents that operate without human supervision. Unlike chatbots that respond to prompts, these agents are given high-level goals and then autonomously execute the steps—researching, coding, testing, and iterating—until the job is done.

The latest generation of agents can handle complex workflows, like writing and deploying code, managing customer service tickets, or even running marketing campaigns. They work 24/7, never sleep, and don't ask for raises.

This has massive implications for labor markets. While the first wave of AI tools replaced tasks, these agents replace roles. Companies are already experimenting with agentic AI to cut headcount. The 'white-collar' jobs that seemed safe are now in the crosshairs.

## SoftBank Joins The GPU Sale

SoftBank has reportedly placed a massive order for GPUs—some estimates suggest up to 250,000 units—in a bid to build out its own AI compute capacity. Rumors say the deal could be worth billions and include both NVIDIA and AMD chips.

This is a bold move for a company that has had a mixed track record in tech investing. But SoftBank's CEO, Masayoshi Son, has been vocal about his conviction that superintelligence is coming, and he wants to control the infrastructure.

If SoftBank's GPU order is real, it could have ripple effects. It would further tighten supply and drive up prices for AI developers everywhere. It also positions SoftBank as a major player in the AI cloud market, potentially rivaling the likes of AWS, Azure, and Google Cloud.
