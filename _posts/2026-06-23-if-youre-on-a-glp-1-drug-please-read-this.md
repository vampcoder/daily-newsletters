Two of my close friends have recently started taking a GLP-1 medication. And they felt a bit scared of telling me.

They thought that I would judge them, that I would think it was a bad thing. But I really don't. I have no judgment about it at all.

Do I wish we lived in a world where our food environment supported our health instead of constantly working against it? Of course. Do I wish these medications had less of a role to play here? Absolutely.

But that’s **not** the reality many people are living in today. The reality is that these medications are helping a lot of people. And because more and more people are using them, I wanted to share a few things that I think everyone should know.

First, let’s talk about **how they work**.

One of the things GLP-1 medications do is increase signaling from cells in the gut called **L-cells**. These cells release hormones that help you **feel full**, **slow down digestion**, and **reduce appetite**. For many people, that can feel like someone finally turned down the volume on food noise.

And honestly, I understand the appeal. If you’ve spent years feeling hungry all the time, fighting cravings, and thinking about food constantly, that kind of relief can be profound.

But there is one thing that concerns me.

When people lose weight, they often assume they are losing *only* fat. **That’s not what happens.** Whenever we lose weight, we lose a mixture of fat, water, and lean tissue. And studies suggest that with GLP-1 medications, roughly **25–40%** of the weight lost can come from lean mass, including muscle. ([read one of the studies](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/))   

Muscle is incredibly valuable. It helps us stay strong as we age. It supports metabolic health. It allows us to remain active and independent. And once muscle is lost, rebuilding it takes effort.

The second thing I think people should know is that **many people regain weight after stopping these medications**. The challenge is that the weight that returns is often mostly fat, while the muscle that was lost does not necessarily come back at the same rate. Which means you can end up with a worse body composition after than before.

So if you’re taking a GLP-1 medication, I would focus on three things.

**👉 The first is strength training.** Not because I want everyone to become a gym person. But because your muscles need a reason to stay. Ideally, I would recommend lifting weights three times per week. It doesn’t need to be perfect. What matters is consistently asking your muscles to do some work.

**👉 The second is protein.** This one is non-negotiable.
