Most people think about legacy businesses as slow. Built on nostalgia, protected by habit, unlikely to change. That framing is not wrong. But it is incomplete.

Indraneel Chitale is the fourth generation of the family behind Chitale Bandhu, a brand most people in western India grew up with. What surprised me is that despite inheriting an 85 year old business, he is building it with the rigour of someone who just started.

He knows exactly which categories he wants to dominate, exactly how much margin every person in his supply chain should make, and exactly why giving his retailer the largest cut is a strategy and not a cost.

The thing that stayed with me was something he said about distribution. His view is simple: if everyone in your supply chain is making money, they will make sure you make money. Most brands think about margins as something to protect. He thinks about them as something to share first.

That is a different way of thinking about how a business actually compounds. Not through squeezing, but through alignment. Eighty five years of Chitale Bandhu suggest he is onto something.

In this week's episode of the Create Wealth Podcast, Indraneel and I cover everything from how Bhakarwadi came about almost entirely by accident, to how this family thinks about wealth outside their own business.
