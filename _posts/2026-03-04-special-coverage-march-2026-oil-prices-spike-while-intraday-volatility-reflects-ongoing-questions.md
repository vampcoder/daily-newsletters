## Key takeaways

- Monday's market reaction was all about higher oil and gas prices, which also caused energy stocks and oil producing countries' assets (including the US) to outperform those of oil importing countries.
- The Strait of Hormuz and the duration of the conflict are key, because there is enough oil supply, but it needs to be able to pass. Risk aversion by ship owners and insurers may effectively lead to a blockage until there is more clarity. And although Saudi Arabia raised exports recently, China has been stockpiling, and there are floating reserves on ships around the world, those will only last for a month or two.
- The risk-off tone and outperformance of defensive sectors were limited, with US stocks even closing unchanged – though Asia's markets are mostly down again today. This suggests that markets are not yet willing to jump to conclusions and price in a prolonged conflict with big economic consequences. Historically, markets do not tend to sell off for long around geopolitical events, and investors should be wary of panic selling. It is important to build resilient portfolios with quality assets, active management and diversifiers.
