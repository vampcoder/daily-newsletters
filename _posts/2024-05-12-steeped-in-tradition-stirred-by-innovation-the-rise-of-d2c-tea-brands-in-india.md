During the previous Diwali celebration, I observed a noticeable shift in the traditional gift choices. Instead of the typical mithais and chocolates, artisanal teas and coffees seemed to have taken precedence. In fact, whenever we receive a gift hamper at home, a box of artisanal teas is invariably included.

This ‘premiumisation’ of the humble chai and the emergence of artisanal tea brands has left me intrigued about the reasons that led to this growth story.

Let’s focus on a bold new chapter in this storied drink’s saga, marked by the rise of direct-to-consumer (D2C) tea brands.

## A brief overview of the global tea market

Tea is the second most consumed drink in the world after water. With an annual increase of 3.3% over the last decade, global tea consumption reached a remarkable 6.5 million tonnes in 2022.

China accounts for nearly half of the global intake, consuming a staggering 46% of the world’s tea in 2022. Both Pakistan and China have experienced the most significant growth in this sector, with annual increases in tea consumption by 6.5% and 8.1%, respectively.

Reflecting its widespread popularity, the global tea industry was valued at an impressive USD 20.2 billion in 2023.

## The Indian tea market

India stands as the second-largest producer of tea globally, as our tea is regarded among the finest due to robust geographical indications, substantial investments in processing units, continuous innovation, and strategic market expansion. Almost 80% of India’s production is consumed domestically.

Assam and West Bengal lead in production, contributing about 83% to the national total. Tamil Nadu, Kerala, and Karnataka account for the remaining 17% of production.

## What has happened in the last few years?

The Indian tea market is undergoing a big shift. Consumers are more than willing to shell out extra money for single estate origin teas, green tea, white tea and various herb infused teas.

Innovative D2C (Direct to Consumer) brands are capitalising on the needs of consumers and reshaping how tea is marketed, sold, and consumed.

These D2C brands are redefining India’s tea industry by juxtaposing traditional practices with modern technology and marketing. Let's uncover how these brands are brewing a fresh narrative for Indian tea.

Here are a few interesting insights –

- In an interview with Fortune India, Kausshal Dugarr, the founder of Teabox, a premium tea brand states that while the tea industry has grown by 25-30% in the past few years, his company has grown by 300-400%.
- Owners of tea gardens whose families have been growing teas for generations are now partnering with D2C startups to reach modern consumers directly.
