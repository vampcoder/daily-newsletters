# Advice for the Class of 2025

Top Substackers share words of wisdom for new graduates—and, honestly, everyone

As the class of 2025 steps off campus and into the wider world, we asked our favorite writers to share the lessons they wish they had learned earlier. Their answers are candid, encouraging, and surprisingly practical—not just for graduates, but for anyone at a crossroads.

Here is what they had to say.

---

### Start before you feel ready

The best way to build confidence is to take action, even when you aren't sure you'll succeed. Waiting to feel fully prepared often means waiting forever. You will learn faster by doing than by planning.

### Question the conventional path

Don't assume that the traditional route—whether it's a specific job, a graduate degree, or a corporate ladder—is the only way forward. Different paths can be just as valid, and often more rewarding. Ask yourself what you actually value, not what others expect.

### Embrace being a beginner

It's uncomfortable to be bad at something new, but that discomfort is a sign of growth. Every expert was once a novice. Give yourself permission to be a beginner and focus on progress, not perfection.

### Build relationships, not just resumes

Your network is more than a list of contacts; it's a community of people who can support you, challenge you, and open doors. Invest in genuine relationships, and don't be afraid to ask for help or advice.

### Seek out challenges that scare you

Growth rarely happens in your comfort zone. That project you're afraid to take on, the move to a new city, the difficult conversation—these are the moments that expand your life.

### Learn to manage your attention

In a world of constant distractions, the ability to focus is a superpower. Protect your time, set boundaries, and be intentional about what you spend your energy on.

### Let your career be a series of experiments

Don't expect to have your entire life mapped out at 22. Treat each job and project as an experiment that teaches you something. Over time, these experiences will point you toward what you truly want.

### Stay curious and keep learning

Your education doesn't end at graduation. The most interesting people are always learning—whether it's through books, conversations, travel, or online courses. Cultivate a habit of lifelong learning.

### Take care of your well-being

Success is unsustainable if you're burned out. Prioritize sleep, exercise, and relationships. You can't pour from an empty cup.

### Remember you're not alone

Everyone feels lost sometimes, even those who seem to have it all figured out. Be kind to yourself, and lean on others when you need support.

---

Whether you're a new graduate or simply at a new beginning, we hope these words offer some direction and encouragement.
