Let’s be honest. Many meetings today can feel like exercises in collective distraction.

You and I have both been in meetings where people half-listen while answering emails or responding to Slack messages.

But meetings don’t need to be this bad. They became that way because they’re too easy to call and too hard to escape.

When I first wrote *Indistractable* in 2019, most people worked in offices. Now, after years of hybrid work, we spend more time than ever in front of screens—not just because of scrolling, but because of constant calls, check-ins, and syncs.

While I was working on the updated version of *Indistractable*, I discovered a few statistics that surprised me:

- Executive-level professionals report spending nearly 23 hours a week in meetings, and many say more than half of them feel unproductive.
- Among professionals, the average time in meetings is rising. Many report 11–12 hours per week as a rough benchmark.
- Large companies could save as much as $100 million a year by holding fewer unnecessary meetings.

So how do we make meetings less awful? Here are a few tactics:

1. **Make sure meetings are for one thing only: consensus.**

   Not brainstorming, not information sharing, and definitely not “thinking things through with the team.” If consensus isn’t needed, cancel the meeting.

2. **Make meetings harder to call.**

   Anyone scheduling a meeting should do three things first:
   - Get feedback asynchronously
   - Circulate an agenda
   - Write a short brief with their proposed solution

   (This small friction filters out 80% of unnecessary meetings.)

3. **Ban multitasking.**

   Devices in meetings make people physically present but mentally absent. Encourage attendees to plug in their phones, close other tabs, or handwrite notes instead.

4. **If you must meet, do it like Amazon or Shopify.**

   At Amazon, every meeting begins with a six-page memo read in silence.

   In 2023, Shopify canceled all recurring meetings of three or more people, banned meetings on Wednesdays, and asked managers to schedule only essential meetings. As a result, it is expected to save an estimated 322,000 hours per year.
