The world is very convincing.

I haven't written a book in five years. I'm happier than a pig in sh*t.

After *The Subtle Art of Not Giving a F*ck* sold 20 million+ copies, publishers started showing up with millions of dollars. They wanted me to write more books, do more book tours, do the whole bestselling author thing.

So I did. I took the deals and did the "author thing." I lived an author's life.

Pretty soon, it felt like walking around in "serious author" cosplay—corduroy, glasses I didn't need, and the sort of smugness you usually only get from driving a Prius.

At first, it was just uncomfortable. By 2022, I was burnt out.

So I took a sabbatical and asked myself a simple question:

"This is all I've ever wanted… So why does this feel so bad?"

The answer surprised me: Deep down, I'm not an author. I'm an entrepreneur who writes.

The way I got here—the blog, the coaching business, the media company—that was entrepreneurship. I loved building things. I loved the game. But publishers showed up and paid me to stop playing it.

I told myself a new story about who I was because the money was good. And I paid for it with five years of low-grade misery.

It's easy to unconsciously change how you see yourself based on what other people reward. Someone congratulates you for being a certain thing, and you start being that thing.

We do this with all kinds of labels.

* "I'm anxious."
* "I'm an introvert."
* "I'm a Scorpio, that's why I'm unhinged."

The moment you adopt a label as identity, you do the mental gymnastics necessary to make it true. And before you know it, you're doing backflips on The Trampoline of Unsatisfactory Expectations.
