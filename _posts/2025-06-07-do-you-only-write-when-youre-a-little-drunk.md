# The Art of Drunken Writing and Other Curiosities

In this edition of the Weekender: reading Proust, the French Open, the volcano that changed the world, and a tribute to Edmund White.

The question of whether one should write only when slightly drunk is one that has intrigued writers and readers alike. It touches on the delicate balance between inhibition and creativity, and how altered states can sometimes unlock unexpected depths. Yet, it also raises concerns about dependence and the authenticity of the work produced.

Beyond this, we delve into the immersive experience of reading Proust, whose intricate prose demands patience and rewards persistence. We also look at the French Open, a pinnacle of tennis that draws global attention, and reflect on the volcanic eruption that altered the course of history. Finally, we pay homage to Edmund White, a master of contemporary literature, whose voice continues to resonate.
