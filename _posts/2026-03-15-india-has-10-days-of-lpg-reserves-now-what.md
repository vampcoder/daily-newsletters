## India has 10 Days of LPG reserves. Now what?

How will rising oil prices affect India, and how exposed are we really?

India, the world's third-largest oil importer, is facing a critical juncture as global crude prices surge. With only 10 days of LPG reserves, the country's energy security is under threat. This article explores the implications of rising oil prices on India's economy and the steps needed to bolster its energy resilience.

### The LPG Conundrum

India's LPG reserves are alarmingly low, with only about 10 days of stock available. This is a stark contrast to the 90-day strategic petroleum reserves that many developed nations maintain. The situation is exacerbated by the fact that India imports over 80% of its crude oil needs, leaving it highly vulnerable to global price shocks and supply disruptions.

### Economic Impact of Rising Oil Prices

The ripple effects of high oil prices are felt across the Indian economy. A $10 per barrel increase in crude oil prices can widen India's current account deficit by $15 billion and push inflation up by 0.5%. This, in turn, puts pressure on the Rupee and forces the Reserve Bank of India to hike interest rates, potentially slowing economic growth.

### What Can Be Done?

To mitigate these risks, India must:

- **Expand strategic oil reserves**: Increasing storage capacity from the current 5.33 million tonnes to at least 90 days of cover.
- **Diversify energy sources**: Investing in renewable energy and nuclear power to reduce dependence on fossil fuels.
- **Strengthen diplomatic ties**: Engaging with oil-producing nations to secure stable supply agreements.

In conclusion, India's energy security is at a critical juncture. The government must act swiftly to expand reserves and diversify energy sources to protect the economy from volatile oil prices.
