I have a small challenge for you this week. It takes 10 minutes. It requires no equipment. It's free. It works *after* a meal. And the science behind what it does to your glucose curve is genuinely fascinating.

This is one of my favorite hacks, and I'd love for you to try it if you haven't already.

## What happens in your body when you move after eating

When you eat something that leads to a big glucose spike (like naked carbs: a big bowl of pasta, a donut on an empty stomach, etc.), your body has two options.

**#1. If you stay sitting**, glucose accumulates in your bloodstream, your pancreas releases insulin to deal with it, and a couple of hours later, you may experience that familiar energy crash, the cravings, the brain fog.

**#2. If you move within 90 minutes of finishing your meal**, your muscles do something remarkable: they soak up that excess glucose directly, *without* needing insulin.

This happens through the activation of [GLUT4 transporters](https://ctrk.klclick.com/l/01KR1JR7TA66DSPCPYZWDZ9GKG_1) in your muscle cells.

Studies tell us that exercise after a meal can reduce the post-meal glucose spike by [up to 27%](https://ctrk.klclick.com/l/01KR1JR7TA66DSPCPYZWDZ9GKG_3). Resistance movements like squats can bring it down by [up to 30%](https://ctrk.klclick.com/l/01KR1JR7TA66DSPCPYZWDZ9GKG_4).

And for most people, that means less crashing after meals, less 4pm cravings, and less brainfog. The bonus: your pancreas doesn't have to work as hard, which helps protect your insulin sensitivity over time too.
