**The Joy (and Frustration) of Trying to Get Better**

Life depends on learning. We spend decades in school, acquiring an education. We want to be good at our jobs, not just for the perks that come with being one of the best, but for the pride of mastering a craft. Even things we do for fun, we enjoy to a large extent because we feel capable of getting better at them.

Yet learning is often mysterious. Sometimes we improve effortlessly—in other cases it’s a slog. We can spend decades driving a car, hitting a tennis ball or working at our jobs without getting better at any of them.

In my new book, *Get Better at Anything: 12 Maxims for Mastery*, I explore the science of skill acquisition, illustrating the core principles that can help you get better at the things that matter most to you.

Few experiences can top the joy of *finally* getting the hang of a new skill. The first time you have a successful conversation in a new language, the first time you hit a tennis ball with proper form, or the first time you debug a tricky piece of code—these moments are exhilarating. But they don't come easily. The path to mastery is often marked by frustration, plateaus, and setbacks. The key is to understand how learning actually works, so you can navigate that path more effectively.

In *Get Better at Anything*, I draw on decades of research in cognitive science and practical examples from my own learning adventures—from learning four languages in a year to completing a computer science curriculum in twelve months. The result is a set of twelve maxims that distil the essence of skill acquisition into actionable principles.

Whether you're a student, a professional, or a lifelong learner, the science of learning can help you achieve your goals. The book is now available. I hope it helps you get better at anything you set your mind to.
