If you had to pass all of the final exams for an undergraduate degree in twelve months, how would you do it? What if you only had a budget of $2000, and had to teach the curriculum to yourself using only used textbooks and free materials you found online?

This was the premise of the [MIT Challenge](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/myprojects/mit-challenge-2/), a year-long project I undertook in 2011.

This project was the first major learning challenge I undertook for my blog. The effort taught me a lot about how to learn things efficiently.

A few strategies that worked well for me, and might help you too:

1. **Watch lectures at 1.5-2x speed.** [Research shows](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/2022/01/17/faster-listening-works/) that listening can be accelerated without major comprehension losses. If you get lost, you can always pause and re-watch.
2. **Learn via practice problems.** Students typically engage in repeated readings to learn material, but careful studies show that this is actually one of the [most inefficient techniques](https://mrbartonmaths.com/resourcesnew/8.%20Research/Memory%20and%20Revision/Retrieval%20practice%20more%20effective%20than%20studying.pdf). You’ll learn faster by doing practice problems with quick feedback from seeing the solutions.
3. **Use the [Feynman Technique](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/2011/09/01/learn-faster/).** Write out an explanation of concepts you don’t understand, as if you’re teaching them to someone else. This helps you narrow down what’s missing in your understanding.

## Could You Learn Four Languages in One Year?

A year after the MIT Challenge, I undertook a different challenge: traveling to four countries to learn four different languages. Together with my friend, Vat, we traveled to Spain, Brazil, China and South Korea. The method was simple: from the moment we arrived, we didn’t speak in English.

How did we do? Interested readers can see [our progress videos](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/myprojects/the-year-without-english-2/), as well as unscripted interviews we held in each language near the end of the trip.

Interested in learning a language yourself? Here are my top tips:

1. **Start having conversations early.** This can be done while traveling, like we did. But it can also be done through services like [iTalki.com](http://italki.com/) or through [practice at home](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/2020/04/20/language-at-home-intro/).
2. **Use [Anki](https://apps.ankiweb.net/)** for spaced repetition vocabulary drills. Anki helps you remember words and phrases long-term by scheduling reviews just before you’re about to forget them.
