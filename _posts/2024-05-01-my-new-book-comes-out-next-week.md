# The Hidden Danger of Overlearning

When it comes to learning, there's a common assumption that more practice is always better. But overlearning—studying or practicing well beyond the point of mastery—can actually hurt your performance in the long run.

The problem is that once you've learned something, continued repetition gives you a feeling of fluency that doesn't match reality. You become confident in a narrow set of circumstances, but haven't really prepared yourself for the complexities and variations you'll face in the real world.

The danger is particularly acute when you're learning a skill that needs to adapt, because expertise isn't just about knowing what to do in familiar situations—it's about being able to figure out what to do in unfamiliar ones.

To avoid the overlearning trap, try to mix up your practice. After you've gotten the basics down, challenge yourself with new contexts, harder problems, or different environments. This will force your brain to build a deeper understanding than simple repetition ever could.

Remember that the goal isn't to practice until you get it right. It's to practice until you can't get it wrong—even when the situation is a little different.
