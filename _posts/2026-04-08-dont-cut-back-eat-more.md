When I was an impressionable teenager and young adult, every spring, I'd always hear the same things, Amol.

*"Ok, time for a diet. I need to get my summer body back."*
*"I'm cutting back on sugar."*
*"I'm eating clean, no more pasta."*

And I completely understand the impulse: spring *does* feel like a fresh start. The sun is out, we can feel summer coming, we all of a sudden have a ton of energy to reach our goals.

But I quickly realized that this diet culture can be so toxic: **Cut. Restrict. Avoid. Take things away.** *(ugh I hate this)*

So what if this spring, we actively tried the opposite?

The Glucose Goddess approach: ***add***, don't subtract.

*(send this to whoever needs to hear it!)*

**The science of adding**

[Studies](https://ctrk.klclick.com/l/01KNPX8AAJHMMKGMTG21G1DKBK_1) tell us that eating vegetables *before* a meal can reduce the glucose spike of that meal by up to 73%.

Not by changing what you eat (you can still eat that pasta). Just by eating extra veggies *first.*

Fiber creates a [protective mesh](https://ctrk.klclick.com/l/01KNPX8AAJHMMKGMTG21G1DKBK_2) in your digestive tract, slowing how quickly glucose from starches and sugars reaches your bloodstream.

Fiber also slows gastric emptying and boosts GLP-1, the satiety hormone, so hunger returns more slowly ([read study](https://ctrk.klclick.com/l/01KNPX8AAJHMMKGMTG21G1DKBK_3)).

That means: steadier glucose, steadier energy, fewer cravings, and no post-lunch crash. All without cutting out on any of your favorite foods.

Interestingly, cultures around the world figured this out *long* *before* nutrition science gave it a name:

crudités in France, antipasti in Italy, fresh herbs eaten by the bunch in the Middle East, or pickles and vinegar salads in Japan.
