There was a moment during this conversation when it hit me: I wasn't just talking to a successful tech entrepreneur — I was learning from someone who'd created 2000+ millionaires and built frameworks for everything.

What followed was a series of structured insights:

- **How ESOPs became Persistent's wealth creation engine** starting in 1999-2000, when employees didn't even know what they were
- **Why rushing into an IPO is dangerous**, and when a company is truly ready for public markets
- **The three CEO traits** he discovered after meeting 150 CEOs
- **His hiring filter for senior leadership**: be exceptional at one of three things
- **His four-bucket wealth allocation framework**: expenses, legacy portfolio, growth investments through AIFs, and impact-driven philanthropy

The thing I couldn't shake off: Anand is still wired like a builder. Still organized. Still thinking 10-15 years out. Still optimizing for time, not tasks.

It was a reminder of how rare it is to meet founders who've created generational wealth for thousands of people and still want to think about the next big wave.

The full conversation goes deeper into ESOP frameworks, when founders should transition out of CEO roles, and why organizing around time (not tasks) is the key to managing complexity.
