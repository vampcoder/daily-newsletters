Back in 2008, I was running 20-person workshops in dust-filled lofts and coffee-stained budget hotel conference rooms.

Every day was a scramble. Post online every day, create a list of cities. When one got enough signups, I’d fly there and start emailing people like crazy to find and fill a room. Rinse and repeat.

I did this for almost two years, living month-to-month, always scrambling to find enough for next month’s rent.

Then, one day I met a guy who changed my life.

*“Instead of traveling so much, you should record one of these and put it online and sell it.”*

Six months later, my first course went online. Only about 100 people signed up. I suddenly had rent money. I could sleep in the same bed for more than a week at a time.

I could breathe.

At the time, online courses were seen as some mix of scam or joke. My friends bought me beers out of pity. My parents told me I needed to go get a real job.

But the world’s information was aggressively moving online. Distribution was becoming global. And you could either move with the world, or drown fighting against it.

I chose to move with it…

For the next 16 years, I would proceed to build out over 15 different online course offerings, five different communities, amass an audience in the tens of millions and reach more people than I had ever imagined possible.

About two years ago, people started telling me they were asking AI all of their life advice questions. I quickly saw the potential for AI in personal development was huge—perfectly personalized advice, infinite patience, immediate applicability, available 24/7—if only someone actually took the time.
