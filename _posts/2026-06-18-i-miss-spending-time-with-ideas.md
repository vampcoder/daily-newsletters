Good ideas deserve more time.

When I hosted the *Beyond Belief* book club, I wasn’t quite sure what to expect.

I hoped a few people would show up. Maybe we'd have some interesting conversations, answer some questions, and spend a little more time with the ideas in the book.

Instead, more than a thousand people registered.

What surprised me even more were the messages that followed.

Hundreds of readers wrote to say they wanted something like this to continue: a place to discuss ideas with thoughtful people, a bit of accountability to finish the books they genuinely wanted to read, and the opportunity to spend time with ideas that might otherwise get lost in the rush of everyday life.

And if I'm honest, I wanted something similar for myself.

Ever since I was a child, reading has been one of the great joys of my life. Books have shaped how I think, how I lead, and how I write. But these days, even the books I most want to read end up at the bottom of my priorities compared to everything else that fills a day.

Still, when I look back, the ideas that changed me most rarely came from reading alone. They came from conversations with readers, authors, students, and friends who challenged my assumptions and helped me see things differently.

A good book can change the way we think.

A good conversation can change what we do.
