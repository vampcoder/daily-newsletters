You may have the idea that sorbet is the lighter, healthier choice for summer, and it does feel that way. But the glucose data says something different, and the physiology behind it makes sense.

**The comparison**

I tested two desserts with a very similar sugar content.

The chocolate ice cream: 24g of sugar per 100g.

The chocolate sorbet: 23g per 100g. Nearly identical.

But the ice cream also has 11g of fat and 5g of protein per 100g.

The sorbet: 2g of fat and 1g of protein per 100g.

Ice cream data vs sorbet data

Fat and protein slow down how quickly the sugars in a food reach your bloodstream. So the ice cream, with more fat and protein, has its sugars "clothed." So the glucose curve is steadier.

The sorbet? Essentially sugar and water. Naked carbs. Bigger spike, steeper crash.

Ice cream spike vs sorbet spike

We know it is the variability (the height of the spike and depth of the drop) that does the most harm to our cells over time. The sorbet, despite having virtually the same sugar content, is harder on your glucose than the ice cream.

**That said**

Neither ice cream nor sorbet is a health decision; they're a pleasure decision. So just eat the one you love the most. And when you can, do the hacks.
