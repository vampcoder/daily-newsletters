**Long Live the Shameless Cloners**

*By Guy Spier*

There's a lot of talk about originality and the importance of coming up with your own ideas. But I think there's a huge amount of power in being a shameless cloner.

What does that mean? It means looking at the people who are doing something well and copying what they do. Not blindly, but intelligently. You study what they do, why they do it, and how it fits into their overall approach. Then you adapt it to your own circumstances.

The problem is that many people feel uncomfortable being seen as a copycat. They want to be seen as original, as innovative. But honestly, originality is overrated. Most great innovations are a combination of existing ideas put together in a new way. The same applies to investing and building a career.

If you want to be a great investor, find great investors and clone what they do. Read their letters, listen to their talks, and observe their behavior. Notice how they think about risk, how they handle market volatility, and how they choose their investments. Then take those principles and apply them to your own portfolio.

One thing that's important is to be open about your copying. Don't try to hide it. Attribute your sources. Say, 'I learned this from Warren Buffett' or 'I got this idea from Charlie Munger.' Not only is that more ethical, but it also builds a sense of community and generosity. When you're open about what you've learned, others are more willing to share their knowledge with you.

So be a shameless cloner. Go out there and find the best people in your field, and give yourself permission to copy what they do. With time, you'll make it your own, and maybe even improve it. But the key is to start by standing on the shoulders of giants.
