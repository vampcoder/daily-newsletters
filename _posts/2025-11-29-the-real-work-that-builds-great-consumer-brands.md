Most consumer brands obsess over the glamorous parts—campaigns, influencers, packaging. However, the real moat stems from the work that often goes unseen: ingredient sourcing, supply chain discipline, and quality processes that rarely make it to social media.

That’s the principle that guided Suhasini Sampath, co-founder of Yogabar. She built the product to global standards before the market even knew it needed them. Every formulation, vendor relationship, and process decision flowed from that obsession with quality.

And that foundation is what ultimately made Yoga Bar the category leader it became.

Together, we delved into:

- The New York moment at a yoga class that pushed Suhasini and her sister to question why India had no clean, trustworthy snack
- How true category creators operate when the market isn’t ready — educating consumers first and scaling only once the demand exists
- Their supply chain discipline— tight vendor relationships, factory-level hygiene, and formulations that could hold up to global standards
- The founder-thinking that kept them grounded: refusing celebrity spends, avoiding premature TV, and letting product do the talking
- Why ITC saw Yoga Bar as a strategic fit: category depth, operational discipline and a brand that could influence the future of healthy snacking

This chat genuinely changed how I think about consumer moats and product-first building.
