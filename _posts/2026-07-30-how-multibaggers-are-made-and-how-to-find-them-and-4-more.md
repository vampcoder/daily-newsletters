**How Multibaggers Are Made (And How to Find Them)**

“How do big investors find multibaggers?” This question obsessed me when I was new to investing. Everywhere I looked, I kept hearing this word again and again: “Multibagger.” A stock that can give 5x, 10x, even 100x returns. Honestly… I was hooked. I thought about little else. I read every book, every interview, every case study I could find. And over time, I noticed a pattern. The investors who consistently found multibaggers weren’t using some secret formula. They were looking in the same place: megatrends.

A megatrend is a powerful, sustained shift that reshapes how we live, work, and invest. Think of the internet in the 1990s, cloud computing in the 2010s, or artificial intelligence today. These are not short-lived fads; they are decade-long transformations that create entire new industries and destroy old ones. The winners in these trends—companies that become the backbone of the new world—are often the ones that deliver 10x or 100x returns.

But identifying a megatrend is only the first step. The real skill is finding the company that will dominate it. Not every player in a growing industry becomes a multibagger. Many stagnate or get left behind. So, what separates the eventual winners? I’ve found a few common traits: a clear and unassailable competitive advantage, a founder who is still driving the vision, and a product or service that becomes indispensable to its customers. These companies don’t just ride the trend; they define it.

Another key lesson is patience. Multibaggers are not built in a month or even a year. They compound over many years, and along the way they will test your conviction with drawdowns, doubts, and doom-and-gloom headlines. The investors who make the biggest fortunes are the ones who buy early, hold through the noise, and let the megatrend play out. It sounds simple, but it’s brutally hard in practice.

So, how do you find the next multibagger? Start by asking where the world is going. Study the megatrends that are already starting to move the needle—AI, clean energy, biotech, even the changing demographics of the global economy. Then, within those spaces, look for companies that check the boxes: strong unit economics, a massive addressable market, and a moat that gets wider every year. And once you find them, be willing to hold for the long term.

That’s how multibaggers are made. Not by chasing hot tips or timing the market, but by understanding the shape of the future and having the conviction to stay the course. I’ll be exploring each of these ideas in greater depth in future posts, and I’m excited to walk this path with you.
