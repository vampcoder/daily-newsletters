## Lessons from the Books

### 1. Friendships need deliberate effort
A recurring theme was that friendships don't just happen; they require intentionality. Whether it's scheduling regular meetups or remembering important details, effort matters.

### 2. The science of belonging
Several books explored the psychology behind social connection. Loneliness isn't just about being alone—it's about feeling disconnected. Even introverts need meaningful relationships, but the way they build them may differ.

### 3. Quality over quantity
The research is clear: having a few close friends is better than having many superficial ones. Deep conversations and emotional support are what count.

### 4. Vulnerability is a superpower
Books like *Daring Greatly* emphasized that being open and vulnerable is key to forming trust. Sharing struggles can actually strengthen bonds.

### 5. Conflict is normal
Friendships aren't always smooth. Learning to navigate disagreements and repairs makes relationships stronger in the long run.

### 6. Context matters
Many adult friendships form through shared activities—work, hobbies, or parenting. Changing contexts can make friendships harder to maintain, so it's important to be proactive.

### 7. The power of small gestures
A quick text, a birthday card, or a coffee invite can be surprisingly meaningful. Consistency beats grand gestures.

### 8. Self-disclosure leads to intimacy
Asking questions and sharing about yourself gradually builds closeness. This is a skill that can be practiced.

## Conclusion

Making and keeping friends is a skill, not a lucky accident. By applying what I've learned from these books, I hope to improve my own connections this month and beyond.
