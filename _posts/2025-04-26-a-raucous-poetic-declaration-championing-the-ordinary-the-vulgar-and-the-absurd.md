In this edition of the Weekender: Benny Blanco’s cookbook, Claes Oldenburg’s Store, and ambient music for spring

## Benny Blanco’s Cookbook: A Love Letter to Excess

Benny Blanco, the hit-making music producer, has released a cookbook that is as raucous and unapologetic as his personality. It’s not about refined technique or delicate plating; it’s a full-throated celebration of comfort food, indulgence, and the joy of cooking without rules. Think mountains of cheese, unapologetic carbs, and recipes that feel like a late-night snack with your funniest friend.

The book’s energy is contagious, urging you to embrace the messy, the loud, and the absurd in your own kitchen. It’s a reminder that food can be fun, irreverent, and deeply personal.

## Claes Oldenburg’s Store: Where Art Meets Commerce

Claes Oldenburg’s historic 1961 installation, The Store, transformed a Lower East Side storefront into a gallery of oversized, painted plaster sculptures of everyday items—shirts, pies, rings, and food. It was a raucous, poetic declaration championing the ordinary, the vulgar, and the absurd.

By placing consumer goods on pedestals, Oldenburg blurred the line between art and life, inviting us to see the beauty and humor in the objects we pass every day. His work feels more relevant than ever in our world of consumer culture and commodified aesthetics.

## Ambient Music for Spring: The Sound of Soft Renewal

As the world wakes up from winter, ambient music provides the perfect soundtrack for this transitional season. It’s not about melody or rhythm but about atmosphere and mood—the hushed drone of a synth pad, the gentle crackle of field recordings, the slow pulse of a minimal chord.

These tracks are designed for quiet mornings, open windows, and the gentle act of paying attention to the world as it greens around us. It’s a reminder to slow down, listen, and let the ordinary sounds of existence become art.
