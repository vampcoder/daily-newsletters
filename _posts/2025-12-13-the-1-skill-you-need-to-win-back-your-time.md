If there’s one skill I wish I could give everyone, it’s the ability to do exactly what they said they’d do without getting pulled off track by a thousand little distractions.

I didn’t write *Indistractable* because I had all the answers. I wrote it because, like you, I was constantly finding myself in that frustrating gap between my intentions and my actions. I’d plan to spend an hour on a big project, only to “just check email” and find myself 45 minutes later deep in a rabbit hole I never meant to enter.

What I learned is that the real enemy isn’t your phone or your inbox. It’s often the discomfort you’re trying to avoid in that moment. We get distracted when we feel bored, stressed, anxious, or restless, and we look for the quickest escape. If you don’t know how to deal with those feelings, all the productivity hacks in the world won’t help.

Here’s a simple exercise you can try today. The next time you feel the urge to check your phone or open a new browser tab, pause for just a few seconds. Ask yourself, “What am I feeling right now?” Name the sensation. Maybe it’s boredom, maybe it’s anxiety about the task in front of you, and then get curious about it instead of running from it.

When I started doing this, I realized how often my distractions were really a way to avoid something uncomfortable. By noticing the pattern, I could start reimagining the moment, reframing the task, or simply allowing the feeling to pass without acting on it. It’s a small shift, but it’s the first step to regaining control.
