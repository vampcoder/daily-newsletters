There was a moment at the Product Unconference when it hit me: I wasn’t listening to Mr Vijay Shekhar Sharma on stage — **I was interviewing him.**

From there, the conversation opened up in a way I didn’t expect. He stopped giving “stage answers” and shifted into the builder I’ve always heard about.

What followed was a series of honest detours:

- How technology keeps compounding in layers, from chips to PCs, operating systems to applications, mobile to cloud, data to AI to agents, and why every layer eventually becomes someone else’s foundation  
- Why pivots are never sudden and happen when founders keep speaking about the future until the organisation’s centre of gravity shifts  
- His one-by-three rule for change — one group moves immediately, one hesitates, one never moves, and you don’t wait for all three  
- Why he moved Paytm to video-first earnings calls  
- The bold move of creating Paytm’s ESOP pool by diluting his own equity when the board was not ready to back the business  
- The toll and discipline of switching from a founder-led culture to public-company governance  
- The pieces of the journey he said still keep him awake

And then we got into AI. He spoke about the Soundbox — a small device that basically changed Paytm’s destiny. And now he wants that same Soundbox to become the merchant’s advisor — telling them what sells, what doesn’t, when to stock up, when to run an offer, and eventually even driving ads for them.

That’s not AI for headlines. That’s AI that touches the kirana shop. The AI that drives on-ground, tangible outcomes for the average joe.
