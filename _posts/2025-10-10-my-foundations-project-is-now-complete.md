One year ago, I started a project to improve the foundations for my life. Each month, I picked a theme, and read books and worked on new habits and practices to support that foundation. Today, I'd like to step back and look at how the project went as a whole.

I've already done updates for each of the twelve foundations, which you are welcome to check out here: Fitness, Productivity, Money, Food, Reading, Outreach, Sleep, Reflection, Connection, Focus, Organization and Service. I also summarized some key lessons from the 102 books I read as part of the project here.

## The Year as a Whole

Overall, I'm pleased with how the year went. The project gave me a structured way to improve different areas of my life without feeling overwhelmed. Each month, I had a clear focus, which made it easier to build meaningful habits and dive deep into relevant books. Some foundations were harder than others, and I certainly had months where I fell short of my goals. But the overall trajectory was positive.

A few themes emerged from the project that I think are worth sharing.

First, **the power of a narrow focus**. By dedicating a month to one thing, I was able to make more progress than I would have by trying to improve everything at once. The constraint was freeing.

Second, **the value of regular reflection**. The end-of-month updates forced me to think honestly about what worked and what didn't, which helped me adjust my approach as the year went on.

Third, **the cumulative effect of reading**. Reading 102 books across a variety of topics was a significant part of the project. I was surprised by how many ideas overlapped and reinforced each other, and I've tried to distill the most useful lessons into a set of principles I can apply going forward.

The project is over, but I plan to keep many of the practices I built into my daily life. I'm glad I did it.
