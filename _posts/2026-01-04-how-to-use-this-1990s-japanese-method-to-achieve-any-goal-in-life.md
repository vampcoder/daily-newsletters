The response has been overwhelming, and a big part of the essay’s impact comes from a remarkably practical solution it presents — the **Harada Method**.

## What Is the Harada Method?

Developed in the 1990s by Japanese professor and productivity expert **Shinya Harada**, the Harada Method is a goal-achievement framework built on the principle that lasting success must be anchored in a clear vision and a deep sense of purpose. It was initially designed to help small business owners and individuals realize ambitious goals, but it has since been adapted by people across all walks of life.

Unlike goal-setting frameworks that focus narrowly on metrics and milestones, the Harada Method emphasizes **self-transformation**. Its core idea: to achieve a significant goal, you must first transform who you are on the inside, then align your daily actions with that new identity.

The method is often distilled into a simple but powerful template with three main components:

1. **Your Life’s Purpose (Vision)** — This is your ultimate goal, the big “why” behind everything else. It should be ambitious and aspirational.
2. **Target Goals (Mission)** — These are the major milestones that bridge your current reality and your vision. They’re specific, measurable, and time-bound.
3. **Daily Action Plans (Behavior)** — Concrete, daily actions that ensure consistent progress toward your target goals. The Harada Method insists that these actions, however small, must be completed daily — not just weekly or monthly.

## Why the Harada Method Works

**It starts with “spiritual preparation.”** In Japanese, this is called *seishinmen no junbi*. Before you can achieve anything significant, you must first conquer your own doubts, fears, and distractions. This means getting crystal clear on your values and priorities. For the goal of building a INR 25 Crore corpus by 50, it might mean asking yourself: *What kind of person saves consistently and invests wisely? What do I need to become to make that inevitable?*

**It blends long-term vision with daily action.** Many goal-setting systems fail because they’re either too broad or too rigid. The Harada Method bridges the gap. You set a grand, 20-year vision, then work backward to create annual, monthly, and daily plans. This layered structure keeps you both inspired and accountable.

**It’s one-on-one and deeply personal.** The method is often taught through personal coaching, where you fill out a one-page worksheet that maps your purpose, goals, and actions. This personalization makes it far more powerful than a generic productivity app.

## A Look at the Harada Method Template

If you want to apply it, you’ll create a worksheet that contains three key sections:

1. **Life Purpose** (Your ultimate vision — e.g., “Achieve financial freedom and retire with INR 25 Crores by age 50.”)
2. **Target Goals** (Major milestones — e.g., “Invest INR 1 Lakh per month in a diversified portfolio starting this year.”)
3. **Daily Actions** (Small, repeatable tasks — e.g., “Review my monthly budget every morning before checking email.”)

Many users find that writing this down daily or reviewing it each morning creates an incredible sense of alignment. It’s not just about what you do; it’s about reminding yourself *who you are becoming*.

## Take the First Step

The Harada Method is already helping thousands of people move from drifting to intentional living. Whether your goal is financial independence, career mastery, or building a healthier lifestyle, this 1990s Japanese approach offers a time-tested path.

So, try this today: write down your life purpose, your top three target goals for the next 12 months, and the one daily habit that will move you forward. Then commit to doing that daily habit no matter what. That’s the Harada way.
