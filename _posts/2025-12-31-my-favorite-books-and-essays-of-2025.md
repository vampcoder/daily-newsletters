Normally, I like to end the year with two posts, one sharing my favorite books and one sharing my favorite essays from the year before. However, since so much of the last year was devoted to my [Foundations](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/myprojects/foundations-project/) project, which occupied much of my writing and included brief reviews of the [102 books](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/2025/10/06/102-lessons-from-102-books/) I read as research for the project, I decided to merge the two lists this year.

## Five Books I Liked

While I did mostly read books on the [twelve foundations](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/2024/07/09/the-twelve-foundations-for-a-good-life/) I decided to cover, I did manage to sneak in some other reading. Here were five of my favorites:

### 1. Proto by Laura Spinney

Long before the Buddha taught the sutras or Homer recounted the Odyssey, there was a language that eventually became the words spoken by both. In riveting style, Spinney tells the tale of how Proto-Indo-European, a language that was never written down, became the seed for the mother tongues of almost half the human beings on Earth.

### 2. Power, Sex, Suicide by Nick Lane

Why do we have sex? Why do we age and die? Why are there two sexes and not one, three or ten thousand? Why haven’t we met any alien life? Biochemist Nick Lane argues that the answer to all of these questions is [mitochondria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitochondrion). In this fascinating book he argues that these powerhouses inside cells are more than just batteries: they are a deeper explanation for many of the seeming mysteries of the universe.

### 3. Food Intelligence by Julia Belluz and Kevin Hall

Most popular nutrition books are not worth reading. However, Kevin Hall and Julia Belluz manage to serve up one that is. In this book, they tackle the rise of ultra-processed foods (whose notoriety today is mostly owing to Kevin Hall’s original experiments) as well as the science of nutrition.
