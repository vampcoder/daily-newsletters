New data from 6,000 knowledge workers: focus is a team sport, not a personal virtue.

Ron Friedman surveyed more than 6,000 knowledge workers for his book *Superteams* and found that teams that built focus into their operations far outperformed the rest. They blocked time for concentrated work, scheduled meeting-free days, and reduced the need for constant interruptions.

While better individual habits can benefit employees’ personal lives, shared norms—like scheduled focus time, meeting-free days, and clear rules for when to interrupt—separate high-performing teams from the rest. The best teams don’t leave focus up to each person but build it into their team culture.

The full article, adapted from Ron’s book, explores these insights further—if you’ve ever sat in a meeting wondering when you’ll get to do your actual job, this is for you.
