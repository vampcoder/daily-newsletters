**A Substack writer’s experience at the U.S. border should alarm anyone who values press freedom**

This is the story of a Substack writer who, on her return from a trip abroad, was stopped at a U.S. border checkpoint and asked to provide her phone, passwords, and social media handles. Border agents questioned her about her political views, her writing, and her association with Substack. They demanded to know why she wrote about certain topics, who paid her, and what her 'real' job was. The writer complied, but the experience left her shaken—and it should alarm anyone who believes in press freedom.

The interrogation echoed a troubling pattern: writers and journalists, particularly those who publish independently, are increasingly subjected to scrutiny at the border. The First Amendment protects what we write at home, but the border is a legal gray zone where, under current policies, officials can search devices without a warrant and ask invasive questions about a person's beliefs and associations. For independent writers, this can create a chilling effect: the fear of being questioned, detained, or denied entry can be enough to make them self-censor before they even file a story.

At Substack, we believe that a free press is essential to a free society, and that applies to independent writers as much as to established newsrooms. This writer did nothing wrong: she was a journalist doing her job, and she was made to feel like a criminal for it. We are working to stand with writers and to raise awareness of the threats they face. We urge our community to be aware of their rights and to learn how to protect their data before crossing borders. Press freedom is not guaranteed; it is something that must be defended every day.
