Don't fall for the **"Do-It-All Trap."**

You don't need to personally complete 100% of the tasks you're responsible for. *A person or simple automation tool can help.* Refusing to offload your to-dos keeps you from doing **what you excel at** and being with **people who light you up**.

If you pay close attention to your tasks this week, you'll notice a surprising number of things you can offload...

## The hidden cost of the Do-It-All Trap

Last month, I tracked my own time obsessively for three days. The results shocked me:
- 47% of my "work" could have been done by someone else
- 23% could have been automated entirely
- Only 30% required my unique skills and judgment

That means **70% of my time was spent on tasks that didn't need my full attention.**

If you're earning $75,000 per year, that's $52,500 worth of your time spent on work others could handle. If you're at $150,000, you're burning through $105,000 each year on tasks that steal time from what truly matters.

## Your first step to freedom

Here's a snapshot of the first week of the *2-Week Delegate & Automate Guide*:

- **Set up a simple tracking system**. Create hourly prompts for three days. Each time it dings, ask: *"What did I do in the last hour that someone or something else could handle?"* Capture it in one running note.
- **Use the 80% mindset**. Don't hand off the whole task. You set it up, hand off most of it, course-correct if needed, and finalize. Think 5% start, 10% feedback, 5% polish.
- **Apply the three filters:**
  - **Unnecessary.** Strike out tasks on your list that you're not obligated to do and that make no difference to your life (put them on an "eliminate" list and avoid them going forward).
  - **Infrequent.** Strike out any task you do less than once a week or don't plan to do again soon.
  - **Enjoyable.** Strike out tasks you could delegate but oddly enjoy (like running errands while listening to podcasts).
- **Pick your first target:** Choose one simple, repeatable, low-stakes task to delegate or automate.

If you stop here, you'll be more aware of where your time is going and naturally start clawing back time.
