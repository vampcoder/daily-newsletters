# Performing Credit: A Simple Breakdown

Private credit is a broad term for lending to companies rather than through public markets. One key segment is **performing credit** — loans made to established businesses that are currently meeting their repayment obligations.

## How it works

Investors pool money into a fund that lends directly to companies. In return, they receive regular interest payments. Since the borrower is already performing (paying on time), the risk of default is considered low, but it is higher than investing in government bonds. To compensate, yields are typically higher than traditional fixed income.

## Who should invest?

Performing credit suits investors with a long-term horizon who can tolerate short-term fluctuations in the value of their investment. It is not a substitute for an emergency fund or short-term savings. Those seeking stability and willing to lock in money for a few years — while accepting modest credit risk — may find it a valuable addition to a diversified portfolio.

## Bottom line

Performing credit can enhance returns in a portfolio, but it requires patience and a higher risk tolerance. Understanding your own financial goals is the first step before considering this asset class.
