Indian offices used to be 5-foot cubicles, white tube lights, and grey walls. At 24, Karan Virwani walked into a WeWork New York and decided the Indian office experience needed an overhaul.

I sat down with Karan, CEO of WeWork India and part of the Embassy Group that built a ₹40,000 crore REIT.

When Karan brought WeWork to India, everyone told him to make it cheaper, dilute the product. India is price-conscious.

He refused. His bet: India has changed. India values quality. People will pay for it if you don't compromise.

That conviction built WeWork India into what it is today.

We also spoke about real estate investing. Karan believes most Indians are getting it wrong.

Even today, Indians want to own property in their name. The tangibility and sense of ownership gives them a sense of comfort.

But the math tells a different story.

Own a floor: 7-8% yield, one tenant, one location, vacancy risk, management headaches.

Invest in a REIT: same 7-8% dividend post-tax, diversified assets, professional management, plus stock appreciation now that SEBI reclassified REITs as equity.

In our conversation, Karan shares:

- The three ways to invest in real estate in India according to your risk appetite
- How Embassy built an entire ecosystem of apartments, schools, office parks to cater to HNIs
- How he scaled WeWork across India without diluting the product
- Why global brands fail when they cut corners on quality
