## What Actually Happens When You Die Without a Will

Imagine you've worked your entire life to build a small fortune—a bank account with ₹50 lakh, a flat in a metro city, some shares, and a bit of gold. You've always thought, "I'll make a will eventually," but life got busy. Then, one day, you're gone.

What happens to your hard-earned money? Does it go to your spouse? Your children? Your parents?

Here's the shocking truth: **None of them have a legal right to your money just because they are family.**

Unless you leave a valid Will, the *law* decides who gets your assets. And in India, that law is the **Indian Succession Act, 1925**.

### The Legal Heirs

Under this act, your assets are distributed among 'Class I heirs'. These include your spouse, children, and mother. They are entitled to specific shares of your property. For example:

*   Your spouse gets a share.
*   Your children get equal shares.
*   Your mother gets a share.

Sounds simple? Not quite.

### The Probate Nightmare

To claim your assets, your heirs will have to go through a legal process called **probate**. This means they need to approach a court to get a 'Letter of Administration' or a 'Succession Certificate'. This process can take months, sometimes years, and costs a significant amount in legal fees.

During this time, your family's finances are frozen. They cannot access your bank accounts, sell your flat, or transfer your shares. This can cause immense stress and hardship for your loved ones.

### The Nominee Myth

Many people believe that nominating someone for their bank account or investments is enough. They think, "My son is the nominee, so he'll get everything." That's a big misconception.

A nominee is just a **custodian**, not the owner. They hold the money on behalf of the legal heirs. They are legally obligated to hand over the assets to the rightful beneficiaries. If the nominee is not the legal heir, they must transfer the money to them. This can lead to disputes and further delays.

### The Cost of Not Planning

So, what happens when you die without a Will? In short:

1.  **Your family faces a legal battle** to get what is rightfully theirs.
2.  **Your assets are locked** until the court processes are complete.
3.  **Disputes may arise** among family members, straining relationships.
4.  **A significant portion of your wealth** goes to legal fees and court costs.

Your family has to navigate a complex legal system while grieving your loss. They may have to take time off work, hire lawyers, and spend months running from court to court.

### The Solution

Creating a Will is one of the simplest and most thoughtful things you can do for your family. It is a single document that states exactly who gets what. It saves your family from a legal headache and ensures that your assets go to the people you care about most.

Don't leave your loved ones to the mercy of a 90-year-old law. Take a few minutes to write a Will today.
