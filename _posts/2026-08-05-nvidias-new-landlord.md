## TOKEN: Nvidia's New Landlord

As artificial intelligence continues its relentless march into every corner of commerce, a curious inversion is taking place. The companies that make the silicon brains powering the AI boom are finding their own traditional roles upended. When the landlord is the one being rented, the entire structure of the technology economy shifts.

The relationship between hardware manufacturers and the platforms that consume their products has historically been straightforward: one builds chips, the other buys them. But as demand for AI compute skyrockets, we're witnessing something different. The companies that produce the most sought-after components are no longer just suppliers — they're becoming tenants in a world where their customers are also competing with them for the same finite resources.

What emerges is a strange new dynamic where the most important asset isn't the chip itself, but the space around it: the data centers, the energy contracts, the regulatory permissions. In this landscape, whoever controls the physical infrastructure controls the future of computation, and the traditional power balance between technology companies has been completely rearranged. The question is no longer just about processing power, but about who gets to decide where that power lives, how it's priced, and who ultimately answers for what it does.
