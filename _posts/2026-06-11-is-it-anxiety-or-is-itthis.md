Low-level anxiety. A mood that tanks for no reason. A foggy, flat feeling on what should be a perfectly fine day. These, of course, have a variety of causes. There's no one reason or solution for them. But **four studies** changed the way I see all of these completely. These studies look at a layer of biology that often goes unnoticed. One that might change the way you see those moments, too.

**#1. The depression link: what one of the largest women's health study ever found**

The Women's Health Initiative followed 87,618 postmenopausal women over 34 years. What the researchers found: the women who ate in a way that created the most glucose spikes (more sugar and foods with higher glycemic indices) were 22% more likely to develop depression. ([read study](https://ctrk.klclick.com/l/01KTVF2TJ7AT7WMKKV2BMVQNMB_1))

**#2. The more it swings, the worse you feel**

In another (much smaller) study, researchers followed 23 women with type 2 diabetes and tracked their glucose variability (i.e. how many glucose spikes throughout the day) alongside their emotional wellbeing. What they found: the more unstable their glucose levels were, the worse their mood and quality of life. ([read study](https://ctrk.klclick.com/l/01KTVF2TJ7AT7WMKKV2BMVQNMB_2))
