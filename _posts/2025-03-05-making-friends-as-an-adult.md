I’m now entering the sixth month of my year-long Foundations project. This month’s focus is on outreach. One of three socially-oriented foundations, outreach focuses on meeting new people and sustaining friendships with people you don’t see every day. Two related foundations, connection and service, will focus on improving close connections and finding ways to help others in my day-to-day life, respectively.

## The Challenge of Making Friends as an Adult

A common theme in books on adult friendship is that while we often have many acquaintances, we lack deep, meaningful friendships. Why is that? Researchers suggest that adult friendships require three things: proximity, repeated unplanned interactions, and vulnerability. In childhood and school, these are provided by the environment. As adults, we have to engineer them ourselves.

## What I’m Trying This Month

For this month’s outreach challenge, I’m focusing on the first two ingredients: proximity and repeated unplanned interactions. My goal is to create opportunities for friendships to form naturally by putting myself in social situations on a regular basis.

I’ve started attending a weekly social dance class, which has the benefit of both a regular schedule and a built-in reason to interact. I’m also making an effort to invite acquaintances to low-pressure activities—like going for a walk or grabbing a coffee—rather than waiting for a special occasion.

## The Power of Small, Repeated Interactions

One key insight from the research is that we tend to like people we see often, even if we don’t talk to them much—this is known as the mere exposure effect. So the strategy is simple: become a regular at a few places, and be friendly. Over time, those repeated small interactions can grow into deeper friendships.
