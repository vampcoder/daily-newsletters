I apologize that it's been 8 months since our last update email (though we have posted consistent updates to the [changelog](https://docs.readwise.io/changelog)). As I'm sure most of you know, the software world has changed a lot since the start of 2026, so we've been working overtime to make sense of it all 😛  But we've got much to share, so without further ado, let's get into it! Some of the new features below were foreshadowed in previous updates such as Global Ghostreader while others are surprises like Quick Lookup and a complete rebuild of the Readwise mobile app.   * **🌎 Global Ghostreader (web/desktop)** — You can now chat with your *entire library* rather than just a single document. The possibilities here are endless, but the core use is getting an answer grounded in everything you've ever saved with citations linking back to the referenced passages. Ghostreader can even perform meta-tasks like moving documents, adding tags, and so on. * **✨ Readwise 2.0** — We rebuilt the classic Readwise mobile app from the ground up: fresh design, smoother animations, much faster, better search, new ways to capture, and more. This isn't about Reader per se, but it's such a leap, it's worth sharing here. * **📱 Mobile Chat** — You can now chat with a document in the Reader mobile app without breaking your reading flow. Making this experience feel seamless on a phone-sized screen required a significant update to the bottom toolbar, enabling us to incorporate additional (much requested) functionality such as swiping through documents and easier deleting. * **🔍 Better Search** — We rebuilt Reader search, again. This means more relevant results, consistent results across devices, and faster speeds. * **📖 Quick Lookup (mobile)** — Long-press any word or phrase to quickly see its dictionary definition, internal usage, or translation. Because this feature uses an LLM, the definitions are specific to surrounding context, terms are defined in the author's own meaning, and translations work in any language. * 🤖 **Readwise MCP/CLI** — Anything you've saved to Reader and Readwise, your AI agent can now use as context or perform operations on. Search across every word in your Reader library, triage your inbox, organize your data, and so much more. Connect from Claude, ChatGPT, OpenClaw, Perplexity, Poke, or any other AI app.   As always, there are many more smaller improvements we'll get into, and countless bug fixes as well. **🌎 Global Ghostreader** Ghostreader now indexes every word of every document and can instantly generate cited answers for you across your full library. Beyond just answering questions, however, Ghostreader can also help you triage your inbox, organize your library, and act as a full-powered reading assistant for you.  **Try it now:** [**read.readwise.io/ghostreader**](https://read.readwise.io/ghostreader)    A few things you can try:   * *“What have I read about [topic] and what were the most interesting takes?”* * *“Help me get through my inbox (or feed!)”* * *“Find connections to this idea from other documents in my library [while reading]”* * *“I remember saving a PDF about [idea]. Find it for me.”* * *“Summarize everything I read this week”* * *“What does [author] argue across their essays I've saved”* * *“What's my best document on [topic]?”*   In our last two updates, we walked you through the evolution of Ghostreader. Like any skill system, you can trigger these manually, let the agent decide when to use them, or customize & make your own. Chat seems here to stay and, as more and more people have adopted this behavior, it's hard not to feel the urge to ask an LLM a quick question while reading. Props to Mati for knocking this out, and Piotr for setting up the infra underpinning it. 📖 Quick Lookup In the transition of Ghostreader from a prompts to chat abstraction, the vast majority of LLM-driven workflows were instantly buffed. There was one use case, which happened to be a user favorite, that would get nerfed: looking up the meaning of a word. Consistent with our approach of elevating particularly interesting LLM use cases into “first class features”, lookups are now built-in with their own bespoke interface.    There are three flavors of lookup right now.  The first is a straightforward dictionary definition. This feature exists in other reading apps, so it's not necessarily new. What makes this implementation unique is that it uses an LLM instead of a locally-installed dictionary (eg Merriam-Webster) and draws from the surrounding context to give you the precise meaning. In other words, you don't have to filter through, say, 6 potential definitions to determine which one applies to what you're reading. But this comes with the tradeoff that it only works online.  The second is a term lookup (sometimes referred to as x-ray). This returns the meaning you would find in a glossary as opposed to a dictionary, but of course very few documents come with a glossary. Behind the scenes, Ghostreader finds all uses of the selected term in the document and then synthesizes that into the author's bespoke usage.  This is a particularly important skill in nonfiction reading, known as “coming to terms” [with the author]. We've long been inspired by *How to Read a Book* by Mortimer Adler who writes at length about this practice. Here's our favorite highlight:  “you will find that your comprehension of any book will be enormously increased if you only go to the trouble of *finding its important words, identifying their shifting meanings, and coming to terms.* Seldom does such a small change in a habit have such a large effect.”  Most reading in Reader is nonfiction, but this lookup variant happens to be especially delightful for fiction too. In addition to neologisms and acronyms, you can use it on a character, place, or invented concept to stay immersed in the story. We added guardrails to try and make these meanings spoiler-free (it draws from usages up to your current position), but depending on what you're reading, the LLM might have some external knowledge, so use at your own risk!  The third and final flavor is translation. Again, this isn't particularly novel to a reading app. But the fun part of using an LLM is it can flexibly handle any language-to-language pair, and it uses the surrounding context to give a nuanced translation.  Props to Adam on our team for owning this end to end!  Let us know if you like it, and if you think it's worth us adapting to web/desktop? 🤖 **Reader MCP/CLI** The new Readwise MCP server (and CLI) allow you to give your external AI agents access to your full library.  We launched this a few months ago, and have been pleasantly surprised at how exciting and popular this has been with Readwise users so far. Many folks have called it a “game-changer”, as it allows your AI to easily access everything you saved, read, and highlighted. This is potentially the deepest and most valuable context for understanding what you care about, powering up all kinds of creative workflows and use cases.    The MCP/CLI offer an abundance of useful tools for accessing your reading data. First and foremost is a powerful hybrid search (semantic plus full text) over every word in your library and highlights. However, there's several other tools to move documents, tag them, browse in lists, edit metadata, and more.  These enable all kinds of use cases that don't make sense inside of a reading app like Reader. Folks are creating personal websites sharing everything they've read, organizing their libraries automatically, creating self-maintaining wikis, building visualizations, and other creative ideas.  All you have to do is setup the MCP inside your tool of choice and then your AI agent will have access to any document you save or highlight you make. Connect within ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Poke, or any other AI app. We've also packaged up some general [skills for Agents](https://github.com/readwiseio/readwise-skills) (eg Claude Code/Cowork, Codex) as well as [OpenClaw](https://clawhub.ai/TristanH/readwise-official).  In general, if you just want a simple, fast, smart agent that's integrated with your reading, [Ghostreader](https://read.readwise.io/ghostreader) should be all you need. If you want to try more advanced applications inside your agentic AI tool of choice, the [MCP](https://readwise.io/mcp) is the best solution. And if you're using a terminal-based agent (Codex, Claude Code), the [CLI](https://readwise.io/cli) gives it unfettered access.  Props to Piotr from our team for shipping the Reader MCP and CLI quickly and performantly. **🔜 Coming Up** **Better TTS Voices** — We know how beloved text-to-speech (listen to any document) is, and we also know that the state of the art has advanced quite a bit.
