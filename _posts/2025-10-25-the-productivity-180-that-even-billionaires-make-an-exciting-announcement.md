A story about a billionaire, a calendar, and a complete change of heart.

In the early days of Silicon Valley, venture capitalist Marc Andreessen (the cofounder of Netscape and one of the most influential investors in tech) wrote a now-famous post called *"The Pmarca Guide to Personal Productivity."*

His rule number one? "Don't keep a schedule."

He believed calendars were creativity killers. Meetings were interruptions. Freedom was the key to productivity.

But thirteen years later, he changed his mind.

In a 2020 interview, Andreessen admitted:

*"I've basically done a complete 180. The typical day for me right now is quite literally following the calendar very closely. I'm trying to have as 'programmed' a day as I possibly can."*

Why the switch? Because when you're juggling investments, a young family, and a company that backs hundreds of startups, freedom without structure becomes chaos.

Andreessen now schedules everything. Not just meetings, but reading, sleep, exercise, and even free time.

*"Free time is critical because that's the release valve,"* he says. *"If you don't schedule enough of it, you get resentful of your own calendar."*

That's the essence of one of the most powerful ideas in *Indistractable*: Plan your day, or someone else will.

Timeboxing is not the same as rigidity. It's about aligning your hours with your values—work, rest, relationships, and reflection—so your time reflects what truly matters.
