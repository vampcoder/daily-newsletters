## Reflections on Reading

As evidenced by my previous months’ book lists, I already read a lot of books. This is largely because I’ve never liked watching TV or movies much, so reading is my default form of entertainment. Yet, when I set out to focus on reading for a month, I found there was still room to improve how I read.

First, I noticed that I often read too passively. When reading for pleasure, it’s easy to let your eyes glide over the words without really engaging with the ideas. This month, I made a point to read with a pen in hand, marking passages that struck me and jotting down questions in the margins. The result was a deeper engagement with the material and a greater ability to remember and apply what I read.

Second, I experimented with different reading speeds. For some books, I deliberately slowed down, savoring each chapter and pausing to reflect. For others, I speed-read, skipping entire sections that weren’t relevant to my interests. The key was matching the speed to the purpose. Pleasure reading can be slow; informational reading can be faster.

Finally, I realized that the biggest bottleneck to reading more was not time, but attention. I’d often find myself reaching for my phone after just a few pages. By setting aside dedicated reading time, with no distractions, I was able to get into a flow state much more easily. I’d challenge anyone who wants to read more to start with just twenty minutes of uninterrupted reading a day.

## What I Read This Month

Here are the books I finished this month, along with a short note on each.

- *Why We Sleep* by Matthew Walker – A fascinating look at the science of sleep and its profound impact on every aspect of health. (I did not read this in one sitting!)
- *The Shallows* by Nicholas Carr – A book about the internet’s effect on our brains, which pairs well with this month’s focus.
- *Deep Work* by Cal Newport – A re-read for me, but it reinforced the importance of focused attention. It’s a book I recommend to anyone who feels overwhelmed by constant distraction.
- *The Art of Stillness* by Pico Iyer – A short meditation on the value of slowing down and unplugging. 
- *The Diary of a Young Girl* by Anne Frank – I’d never read this before and was struck by its emotional depth.

I also read a handful of novels, which I consider part of my reading practice as well. Fiction exercises a different kind of attention and imagination, and it’s a good counterbalance to the heavy nonfiction I usually read.

## Final Thoughts

Reading is a skill, and like any skill, it requires practice and intention. This month reminded me that the goal is not just to finish more books, but to read better. That means reading with purpose, paying attention to what matters, and taking the time to think about what you’ve read. If you want to read more, start with the basics: make time, remove distractions, and keep a pen nearby. You might be surprised at how much more you get out of every page.
