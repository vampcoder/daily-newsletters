# DeepMind Fired Its Nobel Team

**👀 What's happening:** Less than a year after AlphaFold won Google DeepMind a Nobel Prize, the company has quietly disbanded the team that built it. The researchers have been reassigned to other projects, leaving the future of protein-folding research uncertain.

This move highlights a growing tension: corporate AI labs are increasingly prioritizing products over curiosity-driven science. While DeepMind once competed with academia for breakthroughs, it now appears willing to dissolve a Nobel-winning unit in favor of more commercially relevant work.

The decision raises a difficult question: If even a Nobel Prize isn't enough to protect a research team, what does that mean for the future of AI-led scientific discovery?

---

# The Copilot Era Just Died at Disney

Disney has quietly shelved its internal AI copilot tools, admitting they failed to meet expectations in creative workflows. The company found that AI-assisted animation and scriptwriting tools produced generic results that required too much human correction.

This is a reality check for the industry. The hype around AI copilots promised to make every worker more productive. But in fields where originality and emotional resonance matter, the current generation of models still falls short. Disney's decision may be the beginning of a broader retreat.

---

# Claude Has No Bottom Line

Anthropic's Claude models have become increasingly powerful, but their pricing reflects an uncomfortable truth: frontier AI is getting more expensive. As the cost of inference and training rises, so do the API rates — and by extension, the cost of every product built on these models.

For startups, this is a warning sign. The margins that once made AI integrations attractive are shrinking. The days of cheap, accessible model APIs may be fading, forcing companies to rethink their dependence on third-party AI.
