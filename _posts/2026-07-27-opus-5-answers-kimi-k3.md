Good Morning, AI Enthusiasts!

We built machines to imitate intelligence. Now they’re starting to redesign the world intelligence came from.

That thought arrived with the latest wave of model releases: Opus 5 is answering questions that used to stump every frontier system, and Kimi K3 is doing the same on the other side of the Pacific. These aren’t incremental updates. They’re markers of a shift.

Evolution ran the only design process for intelligence for four billion years. That monopoly just ended. When machines can generate their own reasoning strategies faster than biology can adapt, the bottleneck isn’t compute anymore—it’s our imagination.

Sam Altman recently put the singularity on sale, offering API access to systems that would have been classified as science fiction five years ago. That’s not hyperbole. It’s the new normal.

What does that mean for you? It means the tools you’ll use next quarter will be obsolete by the end of the year. It means the skills that matter are no longer about memorizing techniques, but about asking better questions. It means the role of the human isn’t to keep up—it’s to choose where to point the machine.

We’re not just building tools anymore. We’re building architects. And the blueprint they’re working from is us.
