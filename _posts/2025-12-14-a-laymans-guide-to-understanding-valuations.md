I have seen umpteen investors beginning their discussions with the wrong question: "Is the market expensive?" or "Is the stock cheap?" It's the wrong starting point because it assumes there is a single answer. The real question is: **Relative to what?**

Valuation is not a number; it's a relationship. It compares what you pay (price) to what you get (earnings, assets, or cash flows). A stock trading at ₹1,000 might be cheap if it earns ₹200 a year, while another at ₹100 might be expensive if it earns only ₹1.

The most common valuation tool is the **Price-to-Earnings (P/E) ratio**. It tells you how many years of current earnings you're paying for. A high P/E could mean the market expects high future growth, or it could mean the stock is overhyped. A low P/E could be a bargain, or it could signal trouble. The trick is to compare a stock's P/E with its own history and with its industry peers.

Always consider **growth**. A company growing earnings at 30% can justify a higher P/E than one growing at 5%. The **PEG ratio** (P/E divided by growth rate) helps adjust for that – a PEG below 1 often implies good value.

Also, don't forget **cyclicality**. Automobile or commodity stocks have earnings that swing with the economy. Their low P/Es often come at the peak of the cycle, making them look cheap when they're actually expensive. Conversely, their high P/Es at the bottom can be a sign of a bargain.

Finally, **be cautious with market-level valuations**. The overall market P/E can stay above average for long periods. Instead of acting on it, track your own portfolio's valuation and compare it with its historical range. If your stocks are trading far above their average, consider trimming; if far below, investigate whether the business is deteriorating or the market is overly pessimistic.

Valuation is not a precise science – it's a framework for thinking. Use it to ask better questions, not to get absolute answers. When you stop hunting for 'cheap' or 'expensive' and start asking 'price relative to what?', you will never get confused again.
