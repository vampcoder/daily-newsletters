Ask most successful people how they got where they are, and the answer is usually some version of "good timing." Everyone else swears they earned it. We're terrible at seeing our own luck clearly, and even worse at realizing how much of it we could create.

In this episode, Drew and I get into why: the psychology that makes us deny luck even exists, and what changes the moment you stop pretending everything is earned.

We start with Warren Buffett — why a man worth tens of billions says the single most important thing that ever happened to him was where and when he was born. Then there's Napoleon, who picked his generals by skipping the résumé and asking one question: "Is he lucky?" And we brought in Annie Duke, a former World Series of Poker champion who's spent more than a decade studying decision-making, to explain why we keep confusing good decisions with lucky outcomes.

There's also a stretch on survivorship bias that'll make you rethink every success story you've ever heard.

Luck comes in three layers. And the research shows you have far more say over two of them than anyone ever told you.

If you want to take the hand you were dealt and play it better, this episode is for you.
