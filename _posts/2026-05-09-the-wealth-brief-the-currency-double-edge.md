A 4% tailwind most portfolios ignore & what it's really worth over time.

Does Your Portfolio Have a Passport Problem?

Priya and Yash are colleagues. Both are disciplined investors. Over the past few months, markets have been choppy, and the rupee has slipped from ~₹90 to ~₹95 against the dollar in under four months. Priya could see that the rupee's slide was quietly making her international goals more expensive and her carefully planned goal-allocation harder to maintain.

One fine day, she messages Yash: “Portfolio is bleeding, how's yours?”

After a brief conversation, she realised that Yash was a little less worried about the slide of the rupee. His portfolio had held up relatively better, partly because of genuine diversification and partly because the falling rupee was actually working in favour of a little US equity exposure that he had.

Although Priya was diversified across market capitalisations, asset classes and investing styles, geographic diversification had never been a deliberate part of that framework. Now she's wondering if it should be.

Let's evaluate, but first, let's take a look at what's quietly been happening to the rupee and its impact on Priya's portfolio.

## Understanding The Rupee's Decline

The rupee has depreciated at roughly 3–4% per year against the dollar over the last 25 years, almost without exception.

The rupee's depreciation is not a bug in the Indian growth story; it's a feature. A fast-growing emerging market with higher inflation, like India, typically tends to depreciate against lower-inflation developed economies like the US.
