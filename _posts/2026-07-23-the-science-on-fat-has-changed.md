# How the 'Low Fat' Label Fooled Us All

Today I want to talk about a label that has confused us for a very long time.  
**"Low fat". "Light". "0% fat".**

These words started appearing everywhere in the 1990s. On yogurts. On cookies. On dressings. On snacks.

For many of us, the message became: Less fat = better for me.

But it’s really not that simple.

**For a long time, fat was treated like the #1 public enemy.**  
In 1980, the first US Dietary Guidelines told Americans to reduce fat, saturated fat, and cholesterol. So the food industry moved quickly. Suddenly, there was a low-fat version of literally everything.

**But that just created another problem.**  
Because fat gives food taste and texture. So when fat was removed, something had to replace it. Very often, that something was... **sugar.** Or refined starch.

**Which meant these “low fat” products were actually massively spiking our glucose, without us even realising.**

A good example is yogurt: Plain full-fat yogurt usually has around 4g of naturally occurring sugar per 100g. But some “low-fat” flavored yogurts can have 10, 11, or 12g of sugar per 100g. Because sugar is added to make it taste better.

So sometimes, these products end up actually being **worse for our health**.

Now this doesn’t mean fat is always good.
