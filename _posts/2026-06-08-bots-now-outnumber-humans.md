Good Morning, AI Enthusiasts!

Apparently the AI boom has outgrown the internet, the grid, the ad model, and the government's ability to pretend it's neutral.

---

Bots Now Outnumber Humans

For the first time in history, automated bots now outnumber human users on the internet. This isn't just a statistical curiosity—it marks a fundamental shift in how the web operates. Bots are now the majority traffic source, driving everything from search indexing and social media engagement to e-commerce transactions.

The implications are profound. Brands that optimize for human behavior may miss the real audience of automated agents. SEO, advertising, and even content creation are all being reshaped by bot activity. As bots increasingly power online interactions, we need to ask: who is truly in control of the digital ecosystem?

The rise of bot traffic also raises concerns about security and authenticity. With bots outnumbering humans, distinguishing genuine users from automated actors becomes a critical challenge. The web is entering an era where the line between human and machine is not just blurred—it's inverted.

---

OpenAI Wants Washington In

OpenAI is making a concerted push to bring Washington into the AI fold. The company recognizes that the future of artificial intelligence will be shaped not just by technology, but by policy and regulation. With governments worldwide scrambling to understand and manage AI's impact, OpenAI is positioning itself as a willing partner—or perhaps a necessary guide—in crafting that response.

But the relationship between Big AI and policymakers is fraught. On one side, there's a desire for responsible development and safety. On the other, there's a fear of ceding too much control or falling behind in a global race. OpenAI's outreach suggests they're betting that engaging with government is better than resisting it. It's a strategy that could set the precedent for the entire industry.

The question now is whether other AI giants will follow suit, and whether Washington will accept the invitation. The outcome could define the next decade of innovation and oversight.

---

New York Paused the Data Centers

New York state has hit pause on a wave of data center construction, citing environmental and energy concerns. The move is a striking blow to the AI industry, which relies on massive computing infrastructure. Data centers are resource-intensive—requiring vast amounts of electricity and water—and New York is signaling that not all development is welcome.

This pause comes at a time when the AI boom is pushing data center capacity to its limits. As companies race to build the infrastructure needed for next-generation models, local governments are beginning to push back. New York's decision could be a tipping point, inspiring other states to reconsider their own data center incentives.

For the AI sector, this is a clear warning: growth cannot ignore the realities of the physical world. The push for innovation will increasingly collide with the demand for sustainability and community consent. How the industry responds to this new friction will shape its expansion for years to come.
