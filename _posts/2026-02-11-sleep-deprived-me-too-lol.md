Honestly, I’ve been sleep deprived with baby and a little bit stressed recently (aren't we all?). It turns out these two things are doing much more to our body than just making us tired...

Science shows that sleep and stress are **directly** related to our glucose levels.

In a [study](https://ctrk.klclick.com/l/01KH6JQB4Z2PDBGQPERK7ZHRQC_1) of almost 1,000 healthy people, researchers found that people with **poor or disrupted sleep** had **bigger glucose spikes** for breakfast the next morning. Even if they ate the **same** meal as usual. And the later someone went to bed, the worse their glucose levels were after breakfast.

And in [another study](https://ctrk.klclick.com/l/01KH6JQB4Z2PDBGQPERK7ZHRQC_2) of 150 people (who didn't have diabetes but had hypertension), researchers found that those with the **highest cortisol levels** also had **bigger glucose spikes** for the same food.

So I put these studies to the test.

I ate granola for breakfast (which I know spikes my glucose even on good days), and it made my spike ***even bigger*** after a short night and stressful morning. A bigger and longer spike means more inflammation, faster aging, and more cravings... consequences we obviously want to avoid.

***So what are my top tips to help with this?***

**1. Set yourself an earlier bedtime when you can.** I sometimes even set myself an alarm in the evening that tells me to go brush my teeth, put my phone away, and go to bed. Even 30 minutes of extra sleep before midnight can help.

**2. When you feel stressed, do something to calm down.**
