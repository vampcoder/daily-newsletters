## Montana Turns Patients Into the "AI Trial"

**👀 What's happening:** This week **Montana became the first US state** to mandate that AI platforms disclose when patients are interacting with AI instead of a human healthcare provider. The rule requires clear and conspicuous labeling of AI-generated responses in telehealth and patient portals.

**🔍 Why it matters:** As AI tools increasingly handle patient intake, medication reminders, and even diagnostic suggestions, patients have unknowingly been treated by algorithms. Montana’s move aims to shift that dynamic from "shadow AI" to "informed consent." This could set a national precedent for patient transparency.

**⚖️ The debate:** Proponents argue patients have a right to know whether they are receiving care from a human or a machine. Critics counter that AI can improve access and reduce costs, and mandatory labels may slow adoption or create unnecessary alarm.

**🏥 Industry reaction:** Major telehealth platforms are now scrambling to update their interfaces, while healthcare lobbyists are pushing for a federal standard to avoid a patchwork of state laws. Meanwhile, patient advocacy groups are celebrating the decision as a "huge step toward safer AI."

**📉 Bottom line:** As AI continues to blur the line between human and machine care, Montana has just made it a legal requirement to show patients the line. The question is: who blinks first — the industry or the regulators?

## Virus Design Crosses a New Line

**🧬 What's happening:** Researchers have used AI to design a novel virus that can evade existing vaccines — raising fresh concerns about the dual-use nature of AI in biotechnology.

**🎯 Why it matters:** While the research was conducted in a controlled lab to study future pandemic preparedness, it demonstrates how accessible AI tools could be misused to engineer pathogens. It’s a stark reminder that AI’s power to design life also brings new biosafety and biosecurity risks.

**⚖️ The debate:** Some scientists say the work is necessary to stay ahead of natural evolution. Others argue that publishing such methods could hand a blueprint to bad actors.

**📉 Bottom line:** AI can design life faster than we can defend against it. The scientific community is now facing an uncomfortable question: how much knowledge is too much?

## Google Owns None of the Business

**💼 What's happening:** A new analysis reveals that despite powering key AI features in a popular business app, Google owns none of the underlying business — a sign of the strange partnerships forming in the AI economy.

**🔍 Why it matters:** This reflects a growing trend where Big Tech provides the AI engine but leaves the customer relationship, data, and revenue to smaller startups. It may be a smart low-risk strategy, but it also means Google could lose control if the startup pivots or if competitors offer better AI.

**📉 Bottom line:** In the AI gold rush, owning the pickaxe may be enough — until someone invents a better pickaxe.
