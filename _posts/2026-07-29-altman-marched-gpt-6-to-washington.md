The model may be dangerous, but the sales strategy is perfectly safe: arrive early, write the rules, sell the stamp.

## GPT: Altman Marched GPT-6 to Washington

**What's happening:** No one summoned Sam Altman. He flew to Washington on his own and showed the White House GPT-6, a model so powerful it could reason through multi-step problems and automate entire workflows. The demo was part of a strategy to shape AI regulation: get ahead of the conversation, meet with policymakers, and frame the narrative before critics do. Altman has been pushing for a licensing regime that would require companies to obtain government approval before releasing frontier models. With GPT-6, he’s essentially saying, 'See how serious this is? Let’s work together.'

**Why it matters:** Altman positions himself as a responsible steward, but he's also securing a first-mover advantage. If licensing becomes law, existing leaders like OpenAI have a huge head start—they already have the infrastructure, expertise, and government relationships. Smaller competitors would face barriers to entry, and open-source models might be restricted. Welcome to the age of regulated AI, where the race is not just about algorithms but about who gets to set the rules.

## The Agent Army Is a Sales Pitch

The latest buzzword in AI is 'agents'—autonomous programs that can browse, plan, and complete tasks on their own. Promises of an 'agent army' suggest a future where AI handles everything from scheduling to coding to customer service. But the reality is more mundane. Most so-called agents are still brittle, prone to errors, and require constant human oversight. They’re not a workforce; they’re a product demo. Companies are hyping agents to attract investment, but don’t expect your AI to run your company anytime soon.

## Anthropic Leaked the Same Way Twice

Anthropic, the safety-focused AI lab, has now suffered two data breaches in a short window—both via the same attack vector. The first leak exposed user emails and payment details; the second, just weeks later, compromised internal documents. The company claims to have patched the issue, but security experts are alarmed: if a lab that prides itself on safety can’t fix a known vulnerability, what about the rest? It’s a reminder that even the most careful AI developers are still software startups with legacy issues.
